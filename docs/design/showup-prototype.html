    Showup: onboarding, home, profile, pause

    Updated to the Showup mouse brand guide: navy-black base, brand blue #2B50D9, cool grey and soft pink. The mascot is redrawn as a vector with six expressions that change on Home. The “o” in the logo is the app’s loader, and it now appears wherever something is loading.

    User flow

    1 Sign upOne tap with Google. Returning users go straight to Home.
    2 Your detailsTwo names required. Date of birth optional.
    3 Add gymSearch by name or area, or switch on Gyms near me.
    4 Weekly goalPick 1 to 7 days. Rest days are part of the plan.
    5 HomeShow up, hold 15 minutes, see the calendar fill.
    6 PauseUse a pass to cover one week.
    7 ProfileEdit name, birth date, gym and goal.

    UX reasoning
    

      

    • One job per screen. Each screen has a single primary button pinned to the bottom, inside thumb reach.
      • 

    • Google first. It supplies identity without a password, so the only fields we ask for are the ones Google can’t reliably give us as you want them shown.
      • 

    • Required versus optional is explicit. Names are required and validated on blur, so nobody is scolded mid-typing. Date of birth is labelled “Optional” and uses the native date picker, which is faster and more accessible than a custom one.
      • 

    • The Continue button stays disabled until the form is valid, and errors say what to fix in plain words.
      • 

    • Gym search is the whole Add gym screen. Each row shows name and address because chain names repeat across Mumbai. The selected row gets a blue outline, a tinted fill and a check, so it never relies on colour alone.
      • 

    • Every state has a next step. No results says what to try and errors offer Retry.
    • Nearby is an option, not a requirement. Searching by name or area works with no location at all. The “Gyms near me” switch is off by default and sorts results by distance when turned on. Location is only needed later, when you show up at the gym, so this screen never asks for it or blocks on it.
      • 

    • Gamification without noise. Progress is a weekly goal bar, a calendar with two layers (days you showed up, weeks you hit your goal), a weeks-in-a-row count and a mascot who reacts. There are no points, leagues or leaderboards, because your MVP has none.
      • 

    • Home is built around one action. The Show up button turns into a ring and timer that fill over 15 minutes, then confirm the visit. Leaving early resets the timer and says why. In this demo 1 second equals 1 minute, and there is a demo link to leave early.
      • 

    • Pause is forgiving and clear. A pass covers a whole week, because goals are weekly. A confirmation sheet shows how many passes remain before you commit, and the calendar marks the week with a dashed pause style.
      • 

    • Profile edits reuse the same patterns. Same fields and validation as onboarding, a stepper for the goal, and the gym row opens the Add gym screen and returns you to Profile.
      • 

    • One loader, everywhere. The logo’s “o” spins as the brand loader. The same ring shows when connecting Google, searching gyms, checking your location and saving profile changes. It is static for people who prefer reduced motion.
      • 

    • Profile is read-first. You see your name, birth date, gym and goal at a glance. The pencil switches to edit mode, with a large Save changes button pinned above the tab bar.
      • 

    • Calendar moves through time. Swipe right for earlier months, left to return, or use the arrows. Week-goal markers recalculate for every month and for your current goal.
      • 

    • Home adapts to where you are. Use the scenario chips under the phone: a brand-new user, a returning user after missed weeks, 5 and 7 day goals, extra visits and a pause. The mascot’s face, message and the week bar all change.
      • 

    • Sample data: gyms, distances, calendar and counts are placeholders. Dates are set to October 2026.
